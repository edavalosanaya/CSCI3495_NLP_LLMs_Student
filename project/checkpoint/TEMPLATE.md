# Mid-Semester Checkpoint: [Project Title]

**Team:** [names]  ·  **Date:** (Week 10)

*One page, one side. Worth 10%. You present it in a group of four in Week 10, Class 1.*

> Keep it to a single side. The point is that three peers and the instructor can
> read it in two minutes and give you useful criticism, not that you write a lot.

## 1. Abstract
The question you are answering and why it matters, in a paragraph. State the
claim you expect to be able to make by Week 15.

## 2. Method
Models, data, and how you will measure success. Name the baseline you compare
against and the metric that decides whether the idea worked.

## 3. Preliminary results
At least one **real number or figure**, however rough: a baseline that runs, a
first comparison, a plot with two bars. "Nothing works yet" is a result too, but
say precisely what you ran to learn that.

## 4. Risks and what you need
What could stop you finishing, and the one question you most want answered by
your reviewers.

---

### What happens in class (Week 10, Class 1, 30 min)
- Groups of four, drawn from different projects.
- Four rounds of ~7 min: **3 min** presenting your page, **4 min** of critique
  from the other three. Everyone presents once.
- Your reviewers owe you three things: whether the page supports the claim, the
  biggest risk to finishing, and one experiment to run next week.
- The instructor joins one group per round. Pages are collected and returned
  with written comments.

### How it is graded (10 pts)
See `../RUBRICS.md`. In short: demonstrated progress (3), a working baseline
with a real result (3), a clear remaining plan (2), and the quality of the
feedback you give your peers (2).

> Bring your questions to the Week 9 work/feedback time to get input before you
> write this.
